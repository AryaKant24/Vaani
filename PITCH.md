# Vani — A study companion for the visually impaired

### Motivation behind the project

India’s National Blindness and Visual Impairment Survey estimated that around 74 million people were living with visual impairment in 2017, including 4.8 million who were blind. 

In my home town, in the month of Diwali, there is an art exhibition hosted by a foundation for the visually impaired students. I felt a genuine need to help these students by making learning more accessible for them. Vision shouldn't be a limitation to learn. A student must be able to learn and explore as much as his or her heart desires. I hope that my project will help not just students, but all visually impaired people get a fair chance to learn and grow.

*Marvel's Daredevil, which features a blind superhero (who is also a lawyer), was also an inspiration behind this project!*

### How Vani helps

Vani turns text-based PDFs into a spoken, sentence-by-sentence reading experience. A student can pause at any moment, ask a question aloud, and hear a short explanation in their selected language. When the answer is done, Vani returns to the sentence where they stopped.

Books and reading progress are saved to the student’s account, so the next study session can begin where the last one ended. Keyboard controls support reading, navigation, adjusting dictation speed and spoken questions.

### Built around the moment a student gets stuck

Vani brings reading and help into one flow: listen, ask, understand, continue. It supports doubt answers in English, Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, and Kannada, making it easier for students to ask in a familiar language. It has been observed that many students find it easier to understand concepts in their mother tongue. 

The aim is simple: help students stay with the ideas they want to learn, even when reading a page is difficult. By integrating Artificial Intelligence, help can be accessed any time any where.

Vani turns moments of struggle into chances to keep learning—one question, one clear answer, one sentence at a time.
Because every learner deserves the freedom to follow their curiosity as far as it takes them.
